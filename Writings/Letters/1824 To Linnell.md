source: printed in Keynes, The Writings of William Blake (Nonesuch, 1925), vol. 3, Letter LXVII. Public domain in the United States (US copyright term for the 1925 edition expired 1 January 2021). Published for the first time in Keynes 1925, from a photograph of the original; not in Russell 1906.
ocr_note: Keynes dates it only "12 o'clock, Wednesday [1824]." Keynes's own note calls this Blake's first reference to the gall-stone colic that troubled him for the rest of his life, and flags the name "Mr. Laker's" as unclear in the manuscript, possibly "Lahee's" — both are Keynes's commentary and are not carried into the body. Printer's small capitals at the opening word and the signature are set in ordinary case.

12 o'clock, Wednesday.

Dear Sir,

A return of the old shivering fit came on this Morning as soon as I awaked & I am now in Bed, Better & as I think almost well. If I can possibly, I will be at Mr. Laker's tomorrow Morning; these attacks are too serious at the time to permit me to be out of Bed, but they go off by rest, which seems to be All that I want. I send the Pilgrims under your Care with the Two First Plates of Job.

I am, yours sincerely,

Willm. Blake.
