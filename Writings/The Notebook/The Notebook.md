The folder gathers what Blake wrote into the Notebook rather than engraved: its lyric poems from the 1790s and from the Felpham and South Molton Street years after 1800, its private verse epigrams on art and on the friends and rivals who turned against him in the 1800s, and the three long unpublished prose and verse statements he drafted in its pages late in life.

Contents, by the date of writing:

- *[[Poems from the Notebook, 1793]]* — the earlier Rossetti Manuscript lyrics, among them "Never seek to tell thy Love" and "Infant Sorrow".
- *[[Public Address]]* (c. 1810) — Blake's prose statement on engraving and the Canterbury Pilgrims dispute, never published in his lifetime.
- *[[A Vision of the Last Judgment]]* (c. 1810) — Blake's prose description of his own lost painting, the fullest late statement of his aesthetics.
- *[[I say I shan't live five years]]* (June 1793) — a single line.
- *[[Poems from the Notebook, 1800-1810]]* — the later Rossetti Manuscript poems, among them "My Spectre around me night and day" and "Mock on, mock on, Voltaire, Rousseau".
- *[[Gnomic Verses]]* — twenty-three short epigrams and proverbs, among them "What is it men in women do require? / The lineaments of gratified desire."
- *[[On Art and Artists]]* — thirty-four epigrams from Blake's marginalia on Reynolds and from the Notebook, his private case against Sir Joshua Reynolds, Rubens, and the Royal Academy.
- *[[On Friends and Foes]]* — epigrams from the embittered years after 1807, naming Hayley, Flaxman, Stothard, Cromek and Hunt by dashed-out initials.
- *[[Miscellaneous Epigrams]]* — fifteen further epigrams and mock epitaphs.
- *[[The Everlasting Gospel]]* (c. 1818) — the long, unfinished verse sequence on Jesus, drafted and redrafted across the Notebook's pages.
