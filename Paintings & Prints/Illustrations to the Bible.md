From around 1799, Thomas Butts — a clerk in the office of the Commissary General of Musters — began buying a picture a week from Blake: small tempera paintings and watercolours, mostly on Biblical subjects, that Blake kept producing for him for a decade. The set was broken up and sold at auction in 1853 and the pictures are now held across museums in Britain and the United States.
